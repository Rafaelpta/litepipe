No euphemism

What it actually does

Most tools in this category describe themselves carefully, because the description is the part that has to travel. Here is the blunt version, in the order it happens.

01You do nothing

It records your screens.

All of them, not the one window in front. No integrations to wire up, no folders to tidy, nothing to remember to save. Moments are screenshots, kept as video on your disk.

Meetings are transcribed too, but only after you accept. Say no and the microphone never opens.

02You stop explaining

You never catch your AI up again.

The pasting, the screenshots dragged into a chat, the paragraph of backstory before every question: that was you being the memory. Your agent opens the archive itself and starts already knowing.

Read two ways, the accessibility layer plus OCR, so what you saw is not missing from what it knows.

03It comes back written

The writing up stops being your job.

The standup, the monthly update, the follow up you promised on a call three weeks ago. Asked once and answered in your own words, with the moment behind every claim so you can check it before you send it.

Your agent, your account, reading a file that never moves.

None of it leaves the machine. You do not have to take that on faith. One process listens, on the loopback address, and it is the app talking to its own engine.

$ lsof -nP -iTCP -sTCP:LISTEN | grep screenpipe

Why it sees more

The context no integration has

Connectors reach the few apps with an API. The rest is peripheral context: the second screen, the tab behind this one, the terminal still running. That is usually where the answer is.

The connector only kind

Whatever the API hands over

Three or four tools agreed to have an API, so those are the ones it can read. Everything else you did today sits outside it.

litepipe

The whole field, periphery included

The screen is the one interface every app already has. All monitors at once, triggered by a click or a pause in typing rather than by a clock.

  • The thread in WhatsApp, on the web, in a tab
  • The terminal, and what the command actually printed
  • The document somebody shared on a call and then closed
  • The dashboard behind a login nothing else can reach
  • The tab that was open at the moment a decision got made
  • The internal tool that will never have a public API
1 in 5

Measured on a real archive over three days: roughly one moment in five holds text that only the image could give. A tool reading the accessibility layer alone would have found those moments empty.

Bring your own model

Point your own agent at it

The bridge ships inside the app. Claude Desktop, Claude Code and Cursor spawn it as a local process that opens a local file. There is no server in the middle, no account to sign into, and no monthly fee for the privilege of reading your own archive.

The hosted kind

Out of the machine, and back

A hosted server, an account to log into, and a paid tier before your own agent may read your own memory.

litepipe

It never crosses the line

A second program inside the app, spawned by your client, reading a file that never moves. Nothing to log into, nothing to pay.

Before you download

Do not download this if

Four reasons this is the wrong tool for you. Better to know now than after twenty gigabytes.

You are on Windows, or you want it on your phone.

macOS only, on Apple silicon. There is no Windows build and no mobile app, and neither is planned this quarter.

You want your meeting notes written for you.

You get a transcript with speakers and times. No summary, no action items, no owners. The transcript is real and the document on top of it does not exist yet.

You need the archive encrypted at rest without opening a terminal.

The encryption is built and works: a password locks the whole archive from the command line. It has no switch in the app yet, so if you will not use a terminal, treat the archive as readable by anyone holding the laptop.

Your employer needs a certified vendor.

There is no SOC 2 here, no auditor and no company to sign a contract. What there is instead is the source, under MIT, and every claim on this page written so you can check it. That is a different kind of answer, and it is not always the one being asked for.

litepipe vs Littlebird

The same job, built the other way

Littlebird is the best funded version of this idea and it is a genuinely good product: it pays attention to your work, writes your meeting notes and delivers a briefing on a schedule, all of which this does not do. It also keeps your memory on its servers, which is what makes that version work and what makes it theirs. The comparison is not really about features. It is about where the copy of your working life sleeps.

  Littlebird litepipe
Where the memory lives Their cloud, on AWS in the US East region, encrypted at rest and in transit One SQLite file in your home folder. There is no second copy anywhere, and no account that could hold one
How much of the screen The active window, read every few seconds Every monitor at once, triggered by what you do: a click, a pause in typing, a change on screen
How the text is read The accessibility layer The accessibility layer, plus OCR for what it cannot see
Whether pixels are kept No. They state plainly that they are not a screen recorder and keep no video, which is the lighter touch of the two Yes. Moments are screenshots, kept as video on your disk, so you can look at one instead of only reading it
Encrypted at rest Yes, by default, with keys managed for you Only from the command line today. There is no switch in the app yet, and this is the one place they are straightforwardly safer
Connecting your own agent A hosted server you sign into, on the Power and Pro plans, which start at 42 dollars a month A local process reading a local file. Included, with nothing to log into
Who supplies the model They do, metered in credits, on plans from free to over 100 dollars a month You do, on the account you already have. Free, with nothing metered
Where it runs Mac, Windows in beta, plus iPhone and Android macOS on Apple silicon, and nothing else
Whether you can check SOC 2 certification and an independent audit, which is the answer a security team asks for The source, under MIT, and the commands on this page. A different kind of answer

Every row on Littlebird's side was read from their own pages on 11 August 2026: context, privacy and security, MCP server and pricing. If any of it has changed, tell us and it gets fixed.

Use Littlebird instead when you want

An assistant on Windows or on your phone. Meeting notes written up for you, with decisions, owners and open questions. A briefing that arrives on a schedule without being asked. Integrations with Gmail, Calendar, Notion, GitHub and Linear that work the moment you connect them. A certified vendor your security team can approve, and somebody to call when it breaks. Those are all real, and this does not have any of them.

Install

One file, on your machine

Download, drag it to Applications, launch. It asks for the permissions it needs and starts filling the archive within a few seconds.
